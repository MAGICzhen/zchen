About me

Research at the intersection of neuroscience, cognition, dynamical systems, and computation.

I am a researcher at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology studying computational and systems neuroscience. My research investigates how neural circuits support cognition and flexible behavior.

I use mathematical analysis, computational modeling, and biologically realistic neural-network models to study working memory, cognitive control, and neural dynamics.

Research

Research interests

My research spans theoretical, computational, and systems neuroscience.

Computational Neuroscience

Developing computational models to understand neural activity, representation, and information processing.

Systems Neuroscience

Studying how interactions among neural circuits support behavior and cognition.

Dynamical Systems

Applying dynamical-systems methods to neural computation and population activity.

Working Memory

Investigating the neural mechanisms that maintain and manipulate information over short periods.

Cognition

Exploring the computational and circuit foundations of flexible cognitive behavior.

Neural Modeling

Building biologically informed models that link circuit mechanisms with cognitive functions.
